## Why ten more explicit years

The linked five-year base forecast ends with positive EBIT but FCFF of -102.065 million in 2030. Applying positive perpetual growth to that negative cash flow would not describe a sustainable going concern. Preserve those signed cash flows and show an explicit 2031-2040 maturity transition, with first stable cash flow in 2041.

Base revenue growth fades from 18% to 3%; EBIT margin moves gradually from the linked 2030 margin to 12%. Upside growth fades from 22% to 3% and margin to 20%. These are **analyst scenarios**, not issuer guidance, a peer-derived estimate, or an approved student judgment. They require commercial success, capacity and operating leverage. Mature peer benchmarking remains outstanding. No downside recovery is invented: the linked downside fails its 2030 funding test, and the DCF refuses to quietly value that case as fully financed.

Reinvestment includes net capital expenditure and working capital. Its 2030 anchor equals cash capex minus D&A plus change in operating working capital. Infer incremental sales/capital from the modeled 2029-2030 revenue increase divided by this net investment. Fade that ratio to mature incremental ROIC / [mature EBIT margin x (1-tax)]. Each transition year funds **the following year's** incremental revenue at that capital efficiency. This adopts a one-year investment lead during the extension, whereas the five-year asset schedules keep their own timing. There is no claim of a verified engineering capacity model. The first extension's investment is visible and is not set to zero.

In the mature state, reinvestment is NOPAT x g / incremental ROIC. Returns of 16% base and 20% upside are analyst choices; they are not total accounting returns on the opening balance sheet. This use of revenue/capital and mature reinvestment follows the [growth valuation framework](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/littlebook/growthvaluedrivers.htm). Stable growth is charged for; the [terminal-value method](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/valquestions/termvalueexreturns.htm) does not assume perpetual free expansion. Maintenance spending equal to depreciation is embedded in net reinvestment; extra replacement spending needs a stress. Cash compensation remains an operating economic expense. Mature cash tax is 25% with no unsupported loss-carryforward asset.

Terminal FCFF must equal final explicit FCFF x (1+g); the engine checks this continuity. It rejects nonpositive mature profit, growth at or above WACC, growth at or above ROIC, invalid dates and nonfinite inputs. A simple unlevered liquidity screen rolls 2030 cash/securities above the reserve through extension FCFF. This is **not** ten additional fully articulated statements or a committed funding facility; it excludes financial income and new financing and cannot establish actual solvency.

## Meaning of the result

The base scenario first produces positive FCFF in 2034. Its discounted operating cash flows plus terminal value are approximately **negative 105.930 million** at 16%. This is the net present cost of continuing the specified strategy. It is not a negative stock price or a liquidation estimate. No zero floor or profitable terminal assumption is inserted to improve the result. Alternatives such as slowing expansion, selling assets or closing programs require their own cash flows. Cash/nonoperating assets and economic claims still belong in the separate equity bridge.

Upside operating EV is approximately 2,315.389 million, with 56.9% from the discounted terminal. A longer explicit horizon mechanically reduces terminal share; it does not make distant assumptions more reliable. Base terminal share is not economically meaningful because operating EV is negative. The displayed grid therefore reports operating EV, not price targets.

Growth sensitivity changes the final explicit growth year, its preceding investment, and terminal reinvestment together. At WACC above incremental ROIC, more growth can reduce value. The tests enforce the economically appropriate direction in this model rather than requiring higher growth always to raise value. Higher WACC lowers EV across the chosen domain; signed cash flows do not guarantee that globally.
