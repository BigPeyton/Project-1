# Project 1 — Edition A

## Decision and user

**Decision:** Whether to initiate research coverage of Rocket Lab as a possible public-equity investment.

**Decision user:** A young, long-horizon investor who can accept high volatility and the possibility of losses while evaluating a speculative growth company.

## Company and valuation date

**Company:** Rocket Lab Corporation (NASDAQ: RKLB)

**Valuation date:** September 1, 2026

## Initial thesis

**Initial stance: Initiate research.** Rocket Lab operates in launch services and space systems. Its contracted backlog and participation in a growing space economy may support long-term upside. This is an early, high-risk research thesis, not a buy recommendation. The valuation, cash burn, dilution risk, execution of development programs, and timing of sustained profitability remain unresolved.

## Enterprise-to-equity bridge

The standard relationship is:

```text
Enterprise value = market value of common equity
                 + debt
                 + preferred equity
                 + noncontrolling interests
                 − cash and cash equivalents
```

Using the 10-K inputs and an illustrative February 26, 2026 share price calculation:

```text
Share price (February 26, 2026)                  $72.65
× Shares outstanding (February 20, 2026)    567,445,449
= Market value of common equity               $41.225B

+ Interest-bearing debt                         $0.154B
− Cash and cash equivalents                    ($0.829B)
= Enterprise value                             $40.550B
```

No preferred equity or noncontrolling interests were included in this illustrative calculation.

**Important limitation:** the inputs are not fully date-aligned. Cash and debt are from December 31, 2025; shares outstanding are from February 20, 2026; and the market price is from February 26, 2026. This is a mechanical illustration of the standard formula. If the course requires the separate synthetic bridge with a known class answer, that class example must be used instead.

## Consequential assumptions

- Demand in the space industry grows enough to support backlog conversion and new orders.
- Rocket Lab executes launches, spacecraft programs, and new-product development without major delays or cost overruns.
- Its cash balance is sufficient for the business plan, or it can raise additional capital without unacceptable dilution.
- The market price as of the valuation date does not already fully reflect optimistic growth and execution assumptions.

## Key unknowns

- How quickly will backlog convert to revenue, and at what gross margin?
- What is the cost, timing, and commercial success probability of major development programs?
- How much additional equity or debt financing might be required?
- How does Rocket Lab’s valuation compare with relevant public peers?
- How concentrated is the customer base, and what cancellation or contract-execution risk exists?

## Research plan

1. Obtain the September 1, 2026 share price and calculate market capitalization and enterprise value using date-aligned inputs.
2. Analyze revenue, gross margin, operating cash flow, capital expenditures, and cash-burn trends.
3. Evaluate backlog quality, expected timing, customer concentration, and cancellation risk.
4. Research development milestones, execution risk, and the competitive position of Rocket Lab’s launch and space-systems businesses.
5. Compare valuation and operating metrics with appropriate public peers.
6. Define the evidence that would change the initial “initiate research” stance.
